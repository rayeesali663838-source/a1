Performance socks for every activity

What to look for in a sock for running, hiking, river and paddle sports, cycling and everyday training, with a quick comparison table.

Running

Road and track running

Running socks should be thin enough not to crowd your shoes, snug enough not to slip and quick to move sweat away. Many runners like a low or quarter cut with a raised tab at the back to protect the Achilles.

  • Light cushioning under the ball of the foot and heel.
  • A compression arch band to stop bunching.
  • Left/right-specific designs for a closer fit.
  • Seamless toes to prevent rubbing on long runs.
Trail

Trail running and fast hiking

Rough ground means more movement inside the shoe, plus grit, mud and steep descents. Trail socks add a little more cushioning and durability than road socks, and a slightly higher cuff to keep debris out.

Reinforced heels and toes cope with abrasion, while merino blends regulate temperature well over long days with changing weather.

Hiking

Day hikes and backpacking

Hiking boots are stiffer and taller than trainers, so a crew-height sock with medium cushioning protects the shin and ankle from rubbing. Merino blends are popular because they stay warm when damp and resist odour across several days.

Try boots on with your hiking socks, and leave a little room for feet to swell on long descents.

Kayaking & rafting

Thin synthetic socks under water shoes reduce rubbing and dry quickly between sessions.

Gym & court

Lateral movement calls for a snug fit and cushioning around the ball of the foot.

Long flights

Soft, non-binding cuffs and breathable fibres keep feet comfortable on long journeys.

River days

Paddling, canyoning and river walks

When your feet are going to get wet, choose socks that drain and dry quickly rather than ones that stay warm. Thin synthetic fabrics are ideal under water shoes or sandals with closed toes.

In cold water, specialist neoprene socks provide insulation. Rinse everything in fresh water afterwards and dry it out of direct sunlight.

Cycling

Road and gravel cycling

Cycling shoes are snug and stiff, so cyclists usually prefer thin socks with minimal cushioning that won’t crowd the shoe. Breathable mesh across the top helps on hot climbs, and a taller cuff adds a little warmth on cool mornings.
