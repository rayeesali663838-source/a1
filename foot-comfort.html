Foot comfort: fit, moisture and care

How sweat, friction and fit affect comfort, how to size technical socks, and how to wash and store them so they keep performing.

Moisture

Why dry feet feel better

Feet have a large number of sweat glands, and enclosed shoes trap heat and moisture. Damp skin is softer and more easily rubbed, while wet socks can make feet feel cold once you stop moving.

Moisture-wicking socks pull sweat away from the skin and spread it across the fabric so it can evaporate. Pair them with breathable shoes and you have a cooler, more comfortable foot environment.

Fit

Getting the size right

  • The heel pocket should sit squarely on your heel.
  • There should be no excess fabric at the toes or under the arch.
  • The cuff should stay up without digging into your leg.
  • If you’re between sizes, try the smaller size for thin synthetic socks and the larger for thick cushioned ones.

Sock sizes vary by brand, so always check the maker’s own size chart.

Troubleshooting

Common problems and simple fixes

Hot spots on the heelHeel slipping in the shoeBetter-fitting socks with a deep heel; check shoe lacing
Sweaty, slippery feetCotton or thick socks in warm weatherSwitch to thin, moisture-wicking socks with mesh zones
Rubbing across the toesBulky toe seam or tight toe boxSeamless toe socks; check shoe size
Socks sliding downWorn-out elastic or wrong sizeReplace old pairs; try a snugger fit
Cold feet after rainWet socks with no spareCarry a dry pair in a zip bag

This is general comfort information, not medical advice. If you have diabetes, poor circulation, persistent pain or skin problems on your feet, please speak to a qualified health professional.

Care

Washing and drying technical socks

  1. Turn socks inside out to clean the side that touches your skin.
  2. Wash at 30–40°C with a mild detergent; avoid fabric softener and bleach.
  3. Wash merino blends on a wool or gentle cycle.
  4. Air-dry flat or on a rack, or tumble on low.
  5. Store loosely folded rather than balled, to protect the elastic.

Rotate pairs

Giving elastic and cushioning a rest between wears helps them recover their shape.

Check before trips

Look for thinning heels and toes before a big hike or race, not halfway through.

Recycle old pairs

Worn socks make good cleaning cloths, or look for textile recycling near you.

After the trail

Simple end-of-day foot care

At the end of a long day, take your shoes and socks off, rinse your feet in cool water if you can and dry them thoroughly, especially between the toes. Let your shoes air out, and put on a clean, dry pair of socks for the evening.

Keep toenails trimmed straight across before big outings to reduce pressure at the front of your shoes.
