Wet-feet rules

Crossing rivers without ruining your day

Sometimes the trail goes straight through the water. With the right approach, wet feet are a minor event instead of a miserable afternoon.

  • Go thin and quick-dryingLightweight synthetic socks hold less water and dry faster on the move.
  • Wring and swapSqueeze out water after crossings and change into a dry pair when you stop.
  • Pack a dry bagKeep a spare pair in a zip bag so it stays dry whatever happens.
Care

Keep the cooling working

Moisture-wicking yarns work best when they’re clean. Body oils, detergent residue and fabric softener can coat fibres and slow drying, so a simple routine makes a real difference.

  • Wash inside out at 30–40°C with a mild detergent.
  • Skip fabric softener and bleach.
  • Air-dry or tumble on low; high heat weakens elastic.
  • Rinse socks in fresh water after river or beach days.

Multi-day trips

The two-pair system

On camping and multi-day hiking trips, many walkers use a simple rotation: wear one pair while the other dries on the outside of the pack. Add a third, warm pair kept strictly for sleeping, and your feet always have something dry to come home to.

In wet weather, a breathable mesh pocket or a clip on your pack is a handy place to dry socks as you walk.

Ask a question
What makes a sock “cooling”?

Cooling performance socks use moisture-wicking synthetic yarns, often polyester with specially shaped fibres, that move sweat away from the skin and spread it out so it can evaporate. Thin knits and mesh panels add ventilation, which helps feet feel cooler and drier.

Are cotton socks good for hiking or running?

Cotton absorbs sweat and dries slowly, so it can stay damp against the skin. Damp socks increase friction, which is why many hikers and runners prefer synthetic or merino blends for longer outings.

What socks are best for river walks and wet trails?

Thin, quick-drying synthetic socks are usually the most comfortable when you expect to get wet, because they hold less water and dry faster. Always carry a dry spare pair for afterwards.

How do I prevent blisters?

Blisters are mostly caused by friction combined with moisture and heat. Well-fitting shoes, moisture-wicking socks that fit snugly without wrinkles, and changing into dry socks on long days all help. If you have a foot condition or recurring problems, speak to a health professional.

How often should I replace performance socks?

Replace them when the cushioning flattens, the heel or toe becomes thin, or the cuff no longer holds. Rotating several pairs and washing them gently helps them last longer.
